Welcome to the Clinical Documentation Lab

This interactive simulation will help you practice identifying relevant information, writing professional progress notes, reviewing documentation for quality, and correcting common documentation errors.

Training Exercise Only

All clients and scenarios in this activity are fictional. Do not enter identifying information about real clients.

Before You Begin

Strong clinical documentation should be:

  • Objective — document observable facts and relevant client statements.
  • Relevant — include information connected to the service provided.
  • Clear — another professional should be able to understand what occurred.
  • Accurate — do not add information that was not provided or observed.
  • Complete — document interventions, client response, and the plan when applicable.
You will complete:
  1. Identify relevant documentation information
  2. Write a progress note
  3. Review a note for QA issues
  4. Correct a poorly written note
  5. Complete a final documentation challenge

🧠 Practice 1: What Belongs in the Progress Note?

For each statement, decide whether the information should be:

  • Include — relevant information that can be documented.
  • Do Not Include — unnecessary information.
  • Needs Clarification — information that may be relevant but is too vague or unsupported as written.

1. The client reported sleeping approximately five hours per night over the past week.

2. The client arrived wearing an expensive designer handbag.

3. The social worker documented that the client was “clearly depressed” based only on the client appearing quiet.

4. The client reported increased stress related to managing work and family responsibilities.

5. The client said they are “doing better.”

Session Scenario

Jane attended her scheduled 45-minute telehealth session. She reported feeling “overwhelmed” during the past week because of increased responsibilities at work and difficulty balancing work and family responsibilities.

Jane reported sleeping approximately 5–6 hours per night and experiencing difficulty concentrating at work. She stated that she has been using deep breathing techniques discussed during the previous session and found them somewhat helpful.

During the session, the social worker explored Jane's current stressors and discussed time-management strategies. The social worker reviewed coping skills and encouraged continued practice of deep breathing exercises.

Jane was engaged throughout the session and demonstrated an understanding of the strategies discussed. She agreed to identify two manageable tasks each day and practice deep breathing at least once daily before the next session.

Model Answer — DAP Format

Data: Client attended scheduled 45-minute telehealth follow-up session. Client reported feeling overwhelmed due to increased work responsibilities and difficulty balancing work and family responsibilities. Client reported sleeping approximately 5–6 hours per night and experiencing difficulty concentrating at work. Client reported continued use of previously discussed deep breathing techniques and stated that the technique was somewhat helpful.

Assessment: Client was engaged throughout the session and demonstrated understanding of the coping and time-management strategies discussed. Client continues to report stress associated with work and family responsibilities.

Plan: Social worker explored current stressors, discussed time-management strategies, and reviewed coping skills. Client agreed to identify two manageable tasks daily and practice deep breathing at least once daily before the next session. Continue to monitor reported stress and functioning during follow-up.

🛠️ Practice 4: Fix the Note

Now act as the documentation specialist.

“Client was really upset today because her boss is terrible. We talked about her problems and I gave her some advice. She seemed better by the end. Hopefully things will improve next week.”

Example Rewrite

Client discussed work-related stress during the session and reported difficulty managing interactions with their supervisor. Social worker explored current stressors and provided support while discussing coping strategies and possible approaches to managing workplace-related stress.

Client was engaged in the discussion and participated in identifying strategies to address reported concerns. Client agreed to continue practicing identified coping strategies and to discuss progress during the next session.

Client Scenario

Marcus B., age 34, attended a scheduled 50-minute video counseling session.

Client reported increased stress during the past two weeks related to financial responsibilities and difficulty finding time to complete personal tasks. Client stated that he has been feeling “frustrated and mentally tired.”

Client reported using a written task list on several occasions since the previous session and stated that the strategy helped him feel more organized.

During the session, the social worker explored current stressors and reviewed prioritization and task-management strategies. The social worker encouraged the client to identify realistic daily goals and continue using the written task list.

Client participated throughout the session and identified three tasks he plans to prioritize during the upcoming week. Client agreed to continue using the written task list and review progress during the next session.
